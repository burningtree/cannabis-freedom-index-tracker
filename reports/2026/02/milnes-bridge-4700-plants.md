On Tuesday 10 February 2026, Victoria Police searched a rural property at Milnes
Bridge, between Kerang and Cohuna, and four men were later charged while a fifth
man from overseas remained on the run. [@1] Police said five men, all from
overseas, tried to run when officers arrived at a property that housed 10 large
greenhouses, and that three of them jumped into an irrigation channel. [@1]
Officers arrested four foreign nationals, aged 39, 41 and two aged 51. [@1] The
men were charged with cultivating and trafficking a large commercial quantity of
cannabis. [@1] They faced Bendigo Magistrates' Court on Wednesday and were
remanded to appear again on 13 May. [@2] Police said the four men had not helped
identify the fifth man, who they were working for, or how they came to be at the
grow. [@1]

Police allege they found about 4,700 cannabis plants of varying size and
maturity inside the greenhouses, weighing more than 2.5 tonnes. [@1] Police put
the crop's street value at $10 million. [@1] The plants were to be destroyed
after forensic examination, and investigators and forensic specialists spent
three days dismantling the crop houses. [@1] Officers also seized electronic
devices, CCTV hardware and items allegedly used to bypass electricity. [@1]
Police allege the five men tried to escape from the 131-hectare property; Nine
described the same site as 324 acres. [@1]

Acting Detective Inspector Matt Pascoe, the Mildura and Swan Hill investigation
and response manager, said police were looking for an Asian male of mid-age last
seen fleeing into bushland, and that a thorough search had not found him. [@1]
Pascoe said removing significant quantities of illicit drugs was a win for
community safety, and that cultivation and trafficking fuel organised crime
groups. [@1] He said there were no obvious links between this bust and others
near Bendigo and Maryborough two years earlier, and that the investigation
remained ongoing. [@1]
