About 9.15am on Wednesday 11 February 2026, Murray River Police District
officers, with the Southern Region Enforcement Squad and State Crime Command,
executed a search warrant at a rural property at Neimur, about 80 kilometres
north-west of Deniliquin. [@2] During the search, police allegedly located
about 5,000 cannabis plants in dense bushland on the 306-hectare property,
with a potential value of $10 million. [@2] ABC reported the crop was detected
by aerial surveillance before the raid. [@1]

Murray River Detective Chief Inspector Mick Fuller said the plants were hard
to spot because they sat within dense forest, and that people had made efforts
to conceal the grow. [@1] Police catalogued and photographed the plants, then
uprooted and burnt them on site with help from the Rural Fire Service. [@1,2]
Two men in their 40s and two women in their 60s were arrested at the property
and taken to Deniliquin Police Station. [@1] All four were charged with
cultivating a large commercial quantity of a prohibited plant and possessing
a commercial quantity of a prohibited plant, refused bail, and were due to
appear before Bail Division Court 5 on Thursday 12 February. [@2]

Fuller said police used resources including State Crime Command, southern
region support, a police dog squad and a drone, which he said helped ensure
safe arrests and destruction of the cannabis. [@2]
