On 28 February 2026 operatives of the Drug Law Enforcement Agency of The Gambia (DLEAG), backed by the Police Intervention Unit, crossed to Jinack Island in the North Bank Region and began cutting and burning cannabis farms. [@2] The Point reported the raid as a weekend operation in a village that residents and outsiders had long described as off-limits to uniformed officers. [@1]

Officers uprooted and burned large areas of cannabis, and officials said several farms were destroyed. [@1,2] Jinack lies in the Gambia River estuary, separated from the mainland by a creek and reached mainly by boat, a geography that had long kept officers away. [@2] Dubawa said cannabis, known locally as yamba, has been a cash crop there because the island has few other sources of work. [@2]

DLEAG spokesperson Dawda Sanyang confirmed the operation but said he could not give details because it was still under way. [@1] He later described the raid as meticulously planned and said it was meant to show that no part of Gambian territory is outside the law. [@2] Amsay Busso, a Gambian quoted by Dubawa, said destroying the farms without offering farmers another income was "punishment without compassion". [@2]

Under the Drug Control Act, possession of more than 2.5 grams is treated as intent to traffic and can bring a minimum fine of D1,000,000 and 10 years in prison, Dubawa reported. [@2] Neither account reported any arrest. [@1,2]
