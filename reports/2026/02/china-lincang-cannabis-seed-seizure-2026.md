Anti-drug police in Lincang, in southwest China's Yunnan province, have arrested
one suspect and seized 1,988.373 kg of non-inactivated drug-plant seeds in a
case of illegal trafficking. [@2] The seizure cut a trafficking chain that ran
from the Inner Mongolia autonomous region through Gansu province and reached
into Yunnan, according to the China Anti-Drug statement. [@1]

Investigators found the suspect bought and sold non-inactivated cannabis seeds
for profit, which police said was a criminal offence. [@3] The case is still
under investigation. [@2]

China's anti-drug authorities say non-inactivated drug-plant seeds can be
planted and refined into drugs, and that buying, transporting, carrying or
holding them is illegal. [@2] The statement said China enforces “zero
cultivation, zero tolerance” for drug plants, eradicating them wherever they are
found, from yards and flower pots to fields and woodland. [@2] It urged anyone
holding such seeds to hand them to police and warned that those who refuse face
stricter punishment. [@2] Chinese law provides up to three years in prison for
buying, transporting, carrying or holding large quantities of non-inactivated
drug-plant seeds. [@2]
