Koraput district police launched an amphibious raid on 26 February 2026 against a clandestine
hashish oil manufacturing unit hidden on a remote island inside the Jalaput reservoir under
Padwa police station limits, on the Odisha-Andhra Pradesh border. [@1,2] Officers approached
by boat and trekked through dense forest to strike the extraction unit led by SP Rohit Verma.
[@1,2]

The raid seized 1,800.5 litres of hashish oil valued at over Rs 225 crore and 1,000 kg of
ganja worth around Rs 5 crore, along with a full improvised lab: a 28 KV generator, modified
pressure cookers with custom piping, metal connectors, storage drums and nearly 80 litres of
cleaning solvents. [@1,2] One accused was caught at the site while the alleged mastermind
and associates fled into the forest; a 30-hour cordon with boat patrols, six grid-search
teams and drones followed. [@1,2]

Police arrested Allen Jayaraj, 33, of Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala, identified as the kingpin
and main cook, plus five others: Kiran D, 33, an unnamed 28-year-old and Bittu B, 34, from
Kerala, and local facilitators Lalchan Khilla, 25, of Malkangiri and Sadhu
Pangi, 45, of Koraput. [@1,2] A Special Investigation Team was formed to probe the network.
[@1]

## Update

Koraput district police in Odisha arrested six people, including the alleged
kingpin of an inter-state hashish oil syndicate, within 30 hours of storming a
clandestine drug laboratory hidden on a remote island inside the Jolaput
reservoir. [@3] Those held were Allen Jayaraj, 33, of Thiruvananthapuram in
Kerala, whom police identified as the syndicate's kingpin and main cook; Kiran
D, 33, Anand Raj, 28, and Bittu B, 34, also from Kerala; and local facilitators
Lalchan Khilla, 25, of Malkangiri and Sadhu Pangi, 45, of Koraput. [@3]

The unit was concealed deep inside dense forest under Padwa police station
limits along the Odisha-Andhra Pradesh border, terrain police said traffickers
long exploited for its isolation and difficult access. [@3] Acting on
intelligence, police launched an amphibious operation on 26 February,
approaching the island by boat before trekking through thick undergrowth to
strike the extraction unit. [@3] An initial police account of the raid had the
suspects escaping and the hunt still on. [@2] The raid yielded 1,800.5 litres of
hashish oil, which police valued at over Rs 225 crore, and 1,000 kg of ganja,
dried cannabis, worth around Rs 5 crore. [@3] Investigators recovered an
improvised laboratory set-up including a 28 KV generator, modified pressure
cookers fitted with custom piping, metal connectors, storage drums and nearly 80
litres of cleaning solvents. [@3]

Police sealed the island, blocked ferry points and escape routes, patrolled the
reservoir by boat, and put six ground teams and drones into grid searches under
the dense canopy. [@3] Police said Jayaraj, a repeat offender with prior cases
under India's NDPS narcotics law, had picked the secluded island, and the gang
converted bulk-purchased ganja into high-purity hashish oil through a
multi-stage process of specialised chemicals and controlled heating. [@3] SP
Koraput Rohit Verma said the unit had been set up barely 15 days earlier, that
funding sources and forward linkages were under investigation, and that police
would seek remand for sustained interrogation. [@3] A special investigation team
was formed under the SP's supervision, forensic examination of the seized
material began, and teams were dispatched to other states to map the broader
smuggling network. [@3] The DIG said no further details could be released while
the investigation continued and appealed to the public for help under the Nasha
Mukt Odisha (drug-free Odisha) anti-drug campaign. [@3]
