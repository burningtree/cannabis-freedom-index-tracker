On Wednesday 23 September 2026, Police Scotland carried out simultaneous
early-morning raids across Aberdeen, Dundee, Falkirk, Edinburgh, Glasgow,
Ayrshire, Lanarkshire, Midlothian and the Scottish Borders, deploying about 300
officers against a Vietnamese organised crime group. [@1,2] The force arrested
36 people and found 14 cannabis-growing operations, serving 35 warrants at 47
addresses; arrests were also made in Birmingham with help from immigration and
tax officers. [@1,2]

Detectives linked the group to human trafficking, cannabis production, fraud and
immigration crime after earlier work connected Vietnamese offenders to a large
share of Scotland’s indoor cannabis farms. [@2] In one Edinburgh flat near a busy
police station, officers forced entry to rooms filled with plants behind tarpaulin
screens; in Dundee, growers had extended cultivation into a shared loft between
two flats. [@2] Potential trafficking victims were recovered from several
addresses, and investigators said they would examine phones and paperwork to
separate organisers from exploited workers. [@1,2] Chief Constable Jo Farrell told
the Scottish Police Authority board that the operation was the largest
coordinated human-trafficking deployment Police Scotland had run and thanked
partner agencies. [@3]
