A man in the Znojmo district, whose name Novinky knows but withheld because of
the sensitivity of the case, caught thieves creeping through local gardens with
a neighbour, and after a fight they held one until police arrived. [@1] Officers
then caught two more men and found a van in which the group had a sizeable stock
of cannabis apparently meant for sale in Austria. [@1] While there, police
noticed plants in the garden, harvested them all, seized his stocks in a house
search, and a criminal case began. [@1]

The Znojmo District Court sentenced him to nine months, suspended for two years,
for growing cannabis for his own use. [@1] He refused to accept that and
appealed, so a senate of the Brno Regional Court heard the case; at Thursday's
hearing his lawyer asked for community service instead of a suspended sentence.
[@1] He told the appeal panel that cannabis is a herb, part of life, and should
be settled at gardening competitions rather than in a courtroom. [@1] The
prosecutor saw no reason to soften the penalty and called the suspended sentence
educational and fitting. [@1] The appeal senate cancelled the suspended sentence
and ordered him to work off the offence with 100 hours of community service,
obecně prospěšné práce. [@1]

Judge Mária Petrovková said the court had agreed with the defence in almost
everything: he had never been punished, was in essence a decent citizen, and
this had been his only lapse, and he had grown the plants for health reasons.
[@1] She said he now knows what he may do and must not go beyond it, referring
to a criminal-code amendment under which a person may grow three plants at home
for personal use without punishment. [@1] He has fought chronic back pain and an
autoimmune disease of the digestive tract for years, and during the
investigation became a cannabis patient who collects medically grown cannabis in
tablets from a pharmacy at set intervals. [@1] After the verdict he told Novinky
that the tablets help with pain but are otherwise quite different, because the
ritual of preparing what he grew himself is missing. [@1] He said he pays a
co-payment of 20 crowns a gram, with the insurer covering the rest, and that the
state took his stocks and limits growing for his own use while spending large
sums to grow cannabis for him. [@1]
