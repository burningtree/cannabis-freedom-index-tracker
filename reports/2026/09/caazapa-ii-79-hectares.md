On 29 September 2026 Paraguay's anti-drug secretariat SENAD, with CODI-FTC Sur and prosecutor César
Martínez of the specialised anti-narcotics unit in Caazapá, said Operación Caazapá II had ended
after five days in the rural localities of Tava Porã (Cenote), Santa Ana, Apepú, San Francisco, San
Jorge and Emilianore in the districts of Tavaí and Abaí. [@1,2]

Agents reported clearing 79 hectares of cannabis plantations and dismantling 12 camps used for
production and storage. [@1,2] Inside the camps they found 3,975 kilograms of cannabis ready for
sale, which they destroyed. [@1,2] Adding estimated yield from the cleared fields to that processed
stock, SENAD put about 240.9 tonnes of cannabis out of circulation and valued the hit to trafficking
groups at more than USD 36 million using average Argentine market prices. [@1,2]

A mid-campaign report on 24 September described one day of the same operation at San Francisco in
Tavaí: four growing plots covering 16 hectares, 300 kilograms of chopped cannabis in 12 bags, three
support camps, and farm tools seized, with no one detained that day. [@3] The wrap-up did not name
arrests for the five-day campaign. [@1,2]
