Police in Orlová received several reports in February 2026 that cannabis could
be smelled in one of the town's streets, and searched non-residential premises
in a building they had identified after months of investigation. [@3] Inside a
freezer, detectives found 3 kg of frozen cannabis tops. [@2] The material was
stored in eight plastic bags. [@3] A preliminary laboratory test found the
material was mostly THC, the psychoactive compound in cannabis. [@3] The man
legally grew and stored technical hemp, a legal cannabis crop, on the premises
and made and sold CBD products from it, police said. [@2]

Detectives detained him at the scene and took him to a police station. [@3] A
commissioner charged him with growing a narcotic plant without authorisation and
handling it for his own use. [@3] He confessed and said the THC-containing tops
belonged to him and were for his own use. [@3] If convicted, he faces up to six
months in prison. [@3] Police announced the case on the official Police of the
Czech Republic Facebook account in a regional dialect, calling themselves
'benga' and describing the illegally held drug as a 'fajn urodu' (fine harvest).
[@1] The post drew praise for its original and humorous treatment of the case
and opened a debate about drug legalisation and the proportionality of
punishment for cannabis possession. [@1]
