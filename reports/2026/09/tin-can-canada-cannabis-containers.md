Nigeria Customs Service officers at Tin Can Island Port in Lagos found 1.2
tonnes of suspected cannabis products in two 40-foot containers imported from
Canada. [@1] The drugs were concealed among imported vehicles and automobile
spare parts. [@1]

The first container, MSDU655132/0, was intercepted on 2 September 2026 and held
890 packs of Delta-8 cannabis-infused cookies and gummies weighing 598.08 kg,
alongside a 2008 Mercedes-Benz and a Nissan Infiniti. [@1] The second,
SKU625643/6, was intercepted on 16 September and held 1,207 packages of Cannabis
Indica weighing 603.75 kg. [@1] It also carried two used Toyota Sienna vehicles,
two used Toyota Corolla vehicles and used engine spare parts. [@1] The two
seizures weighed 1,201.83 kg in total and Customs put their combined street
value at N3.674 billion. [@1]

Customs Area Controller Joseph Anani said the first seizure followed a joint 100
per cent physical examination by Customs and NDLEA officers. [@2] He said the
substances would be formally handed over to the NDLEA, Nigeria's anti-narcotics
agency, for further investigation. [@1] Solomon Omotosho, commander of the
NDLEA's Tin Can Island Port Strategic Command, warned that unsuspecting
consumers could buy and consume the cookies and gummies without knowing they
contained psychoactive substances. [@1]
