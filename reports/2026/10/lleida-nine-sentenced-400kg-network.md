Nine men were sentenced to a combined total of more than 30 years in prison and
€4.05 million in fines. [@1] The Provincial Court of Lleida delivered the
sentences orally at the hearing on 29 October. [@3] All nine were convicted of
drug-related public health offences and membership of a criminal group. [@1] The
plea agreement with prosecutors cut sentences that had initially been sought at
80 years in prison and €60 million in fines. [@2] The convictions are final; the
parties said they would not appeal. [@2] The court applied a mitigating factor
for undue delays, and two defendants, including the leader, also for drug
addiction. [@1] The leader received four years and ten months in prison and a
€650,000 fine. [@1] The other eight accepted terms of between two years and four
years and four months, with fines of up to €600,000. [@3]

The network operated from at least November 2021 to March 2023, using transport
companies, industrial warehouses and commercial vehicles to move drugs under the
cover of legitimate deliveries. [@1] Its leader administered two transport
companies subcontracted by Correos Express, giving him access to commercial
vehicles and logistical infrastructure. [@1] On 8 November 2021, Catalan police,
the Mossos d'Esquadra, intercepted a van in an industrial estate near the N-260
in La Seu d'Urgell. [@1] Two pallets in the van held 29.16 kg of marijuana and
277.64 kg of hashish bound for Lyon. [@1] Police put the retail value of that
load at €1.94 million. [@1] On 13 January 2023, 94.06 kg of marijuana buds were
loaded into a van at a warehouse in Roquetes and seized on the AP-7 near
L'Ampolla. [@2] On 26 February 2023, officers stopped an Audi A5 in Barcelona
carrying about 1 kg of cocaine of 87.8% purity towards Ibiza. [@1] The case's
seizures totalled more than 401 kg: about 277.64 kg of hashish, 123.5 kg of
marijuana and 1.04 kg of cocaine, plus small amounts of MDMA. [@2] The driver of
the Roquetes van was never found and was not covered by the plea agreement. [@1]

The investigation was handled by the Court of La Seu d'Urgell because the 2021
interception took place there. [@1] La Seu d'Urgell became the judicial centre
of a prosecution involving warehouses in Tarragona, vehicles travelling towards
Ibiza and consignments intended for destinations beyond Spain. [@1]
