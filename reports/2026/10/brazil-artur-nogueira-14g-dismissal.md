Alexandre de Moraes, a justice of Brazil's Supreme Court (STF), ordered a
drug-trafficking prosecution dismissed and the immediate release of a man
arrested during Operation Tonsor in São Paulo state. [@1] The man had been held
in preventive detention after a search warrant turned up 14.4 grams of cannabis
products — 8.5 grams of “Flor” and 5.9 grams of “Dry” — a precision scale and
6,247 reais in cash. [@1] The case was before the 1st Judicial Court of the
Artur Nogueira judicial district. [@1]

The arrest was justified by suspicion of involvement in drug dealing, the cash
seized and the risk that evidence would be destroyed. [@1] Although the man had
no criminal record, the court considered other precautionary measures
insufficient. [@1] His defence appealed to the São Paulo Court of Justice, which
denied an interim order, and then to the Superior Court of Justice, which
declined to hear the case citing the STF's Súmula 691. [@1]

At the Supreme Court, the defence argued that the conditions for preventive
detention were absent and asked for his release, with or without alternative
measures. [@1] Moraes noted that 14.4 grams falls within the 40-gram, six-plant
limit the STF set in RE 635.659 (Tema 506) as a rebuttable presumption of
personal use. [@1] The minister wrote that the search of the shop found no drugs
and no material evidence that the premises were used to store or distribute
drugs. [@1]

The investigation had pointed to possible cocaine sales using the vehicle and
shop under investigation, but Moraes said the searches found no cocaine, no
drugs in the car or shop, and no sales records, trafficking accounts or other
evidence of distribution. [@1] He concluded that possession for personal use was
not a criminal offence and that there was no robust material evidence of intent
to sell, and ordered the prosecution dismissed. [@1] The decision was made
available on Tuesday 27 October 2026. [@1]
