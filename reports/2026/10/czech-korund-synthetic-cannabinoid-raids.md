Czech police detained and charged three foreign nationals as members of an
organised crime group and placed all three in pre-trial custody. [@1] Detectives
also opened criminal proceedings against three legal entities. [@2] The three
are accused of unauthorised production and handling of scheduled psychoactive
substances, spreading drug addiction and legalising the proceeds of crime;
police said a conviction would bring years in prison and forfeiture of the
seized property. [@1]

During a series of operations, officers of the National Drug Centre (NPC),
Czechia's anti-narcotics unit, filled 36 trucks with seized products, raw
materials and production equipment. [@1] Police listed 11 tonnes of cannabis dry
matter partly infused with synthetic cannabinoids, more than three tonnes of
cannabis plants, 2.65 tonnes of hashish, 100 kg of cannabis powder, a
large-capacity grow, nearly a tonne of jelly sweets along with the industrial
line that made them, about 120,000 original packages and hundreds of kilograms
of products ready for packing. [@1] Officers also seized hundreds of thousands
of joints, thousands of electronic cigarettes, hundreds of vapourisers and
refills, and thousands of litres of chemicals. [@2] Police estimated the total
value of the seized goods, including production technology, at 330 million
koruna, and seized almost 60 million koruna in cash, bank accounts,
cryptocurrency and precious metals as proceeds of crime. [@2]

The raids took place in Prague and the Central Bohemian, Moravian-Silesian and
Plzeň regions. [@1] Police withheld the exact sites and dates because the
investigation continues. [@1] The raids took place at the turn of June and July
2026. [@3] The operation was code-named Korund. [@2] Officers found two illegal
laboratories for developing new synthetic substances and an industrial plant
where technical hemp, industrial cannabis, was mixed, sprayed, dried and packed.
[@2] The products reached convenience stores, vending machines and shops across
Czechia, where police and hospital laboratories found banned substances that
they say caused hundreds of intoxications, including in children. [@1] One case
was handed to Polish investigators because a substantial part of the crime took
place there. [@2] NPC director Jakub Frydrych called it one of the largest
operations in the unit's 35 years. [@1]
