Michael Perry, a 42-year-old father of two with an address at Mornington
Heights, Trim, Co. Meath, pleaded guilty at Dublin Circuit Criminal Court to
possession of drugs for sale or supply at Kilshane Avenue, Dublin 15, on 1
February 2022. [@1] He was before the court on Friday 30 October 2026. [@2]
Judge Martin Nolan imposed a sentence of three years and nine months. [@1] Perry
had no previous convictions. [@1] He had worked at the transport company Hales
Freight since 2016, starting as a forklift driver and rising to warehouse
manager with round-the-clock access to the premises. [@1]

Gardaí, the Irish police, were called to the warehouse when a Customs and Excise
sniffer dog brought its handler to Perry’s locker, during a search prompted by
unofficial parcels arriving from the UK in the name of a former employee who was
not involved. [@1] Perry gave officers the key, and two sealed boxes were found:
one held five 1kg bags of cannabis herb and the other held 10 bags. [@1] The
drugs were worth €300,000. [@1] Perry told gardaí in interview that the activity
had been going on since the start of that year. [@1]

Gardaí viewed CCTV footage showing that another person had also handled the
packages, and that person is still before the courts. [@1] Defending, Dominic
McGinn SC argued that exceptional circumstances, chiefly Perry’s early guilty
plea, would allow Judge Nolan to depart from the mandatory minimum sentence of
10 years. [@1] The court heard Perry had a drug addiction and that those who
involved him found it useful that he worked in haulage. [@1] Counsel said Perry
had flown back to Ireland to face up to his responsibilities. [@1] He said
Perry’s children would be the innocent victims of his offending. [@1] Judge
Nolan said he assumed Perry had owed money and might have been coerced,
describing those he stored the drugs for as “insidious third parties”. [@1]
Perry said: “I’m sorry I got mixed in with the wrong people, and I’m paying the
price now.” [@1]
