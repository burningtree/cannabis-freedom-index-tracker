The Narcotics Control Bureau, the Indo-Tibetan Border Police, Himachal Pradesh police and the
state forest department together eradicated illegal cannabis across about 592 acres (around 240
hectares) in Kullu district's Parvati Valley over two months. [@1,2] The sweep aims to dismantle
the supply chain by destroying cultivation before harvest, and to stop drugs sourced elsewhere
being sold as Parvati Valley hash. [@1,2]

The plantations hid in densely forested terrain at elevations of 2,100 to 3,050 metres, beyond
the reach of vehicles. [@1] Teams scaled steep slopes on foot through heavy rain and dense fog,
then cut the plants down and destroyed them on the spot, recording photographic evidence and GPS
locations. [@1]

Police say nearby villagers will face less exposure to the criminal networks behind the
cultivation, and that the heavier security presence should curb unlawful activity in these remote
pockets. [@1] The same high-altitude forests that gave the growers seclusion made the operation
exceptionally demanding for the teams sent in. [@1]
