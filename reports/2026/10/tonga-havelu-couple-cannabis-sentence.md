Folauhola Palanite and his wife Mele lived in Havelu. [@1] Acting Justice Langi
sentenced them at the Supreme Court in Nuku'alofa on 22 October 2026. [@1]
Folauhola was sentenced to 21 months in prison, with the final six months
suspended for two years. [@1] Mele was sentenced to 15 months in prison, fully
suspended for two years, on conditions including a drug awareness course and 60
hours of community work. [@1] Both had pleaded guilty to three charges:
possessing 40.07 grams of cannabis, possessing another 3.89 grams, and unlawful
possession of a bong and 150 empty packets used for smoking and packing drugs.
[@1]

Police said two Havelu residents had been arrested and charged after an
operation on 29 January 2026. [@2] The court heard that police received
information that evening that Folauhola, also known as Tusa, was selling
cannabis in Havelu. [@1] An officer posing as a buyer arranged to purchase $100
worth of cannabis, and Folauhola told him to wait at the taxi stand in Havelu,
opposite Liahona Middle School. [@1] Mele arrived in a red vehicle with two
children in the back, and identified a packet containing cannabis when police
stopped and searched the vehicle. [@1] A search of the couple's home found more
cannabis, the 150 empty packets and a homemade bong hidden in their bedroom.
[@1] Mele denied knowledge of the drugs and said her husband had told her to
take a package from a tree and deliver it. [@1] Mele was arrested and taken into
custody at the Central Police Station. [@1] Folauhola was arrested the next day,
30 January, when he went to the station to ask about her and admitted the
cannabis was his. [@1]

The Crown said the quantity of cannabis and the 150 empty packets suggested the
couple were preparing and supplying the drug, and that the amount raised a
statutory presumption of supply. [@1] The judge called the 40 grams on the first
count a serious aggravating factor, saying the sheer amount clearly indicated an
intent to supply. [@1] Folauhola had 14 previous convictions, mostly for
housebreaking and theft, the last in 2022. [@1] The judge said his record showed
a disregard for the opportunities the courts had given him. [@1] He agreed with
the Crown that the long list of previous convictions meant Folauhola was not
entitled to a full suspension. [@1] Mele had no previous convictions. [@1] The
Crown submitted that her whole sentence be suspended on conditions. [@1]
