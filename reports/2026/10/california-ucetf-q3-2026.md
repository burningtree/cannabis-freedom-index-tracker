Fourteen people were arrested and nine firearms and $29,215 in cash were
confiscated in the third quarter's operations across nine California counties.
[@2] Six of the arrests came in a multiagency operation near the Lake Oroville
State Recreation Area in Butte County, where officers also seized or destroyed
nearly 2,700 pounds of cannabis and eradicated more than 5,300 plants. [@1]

Between July and September, authorities seized or destroyed 55,308 pounds of
cannabis, about 25,100 kilograms, and eradicated 108,226 plants, an estimated
$91 million in product by the state's count. [@2] For January to September, the
task force seized or destroyed 139,293 pounds of cannabis and eradicated 236,846
plants. [@2] The governor's office valued that nine-month haul at more than $230
million. [@1] Since the task force was established in 2022, the state reports
having seized or destroyed an estimated $1.47 billion worth of illicit cannabis,
896,514 pounds in all, and eradicated 1,441,826 plants. [@2]

In Alameda County, officers seized more than 19,300 pounds and eradicated more
than 38,500 plants at indoor cultivation sites in Hayward and Oakland. [@2]
Investigators reported suspected connections to organized criminal networks,
along with utility theft, electrical hazards and unregistered pesticides. [@2] A
three-day operation near Lancaster in Los Angeles County resulted in about
16,760 pounds seized and more than 33,000 plants eradicated. [@2] Officers there
also recovered firearms and methamphetamine and documented environmental
concerns in nearby desert habitat. [@2] Operations in Mendocino County added
more than 4,400 pounds seized or destroyed and nearly 7,000 plants eradicated.
[@2]

The task force is co-chaired by the Department of Cannabis Control and the
Department of Fish and Wildlife, with coordination from the Governor's Office of
Emergency Services. [@1] Its investigations also address illegal water
diversions, hazardous pesticides, suspected labor exploitation and other
violations tied to unlicensed marijuana operations. [@2] The figures reflect
seizures and estimated product values, not actual sales or revenue generated by
illegal operators. [@2]
