Spain's Policía Nacional arrested 11 people in Benidorm — seven men and four
women aged between 18 and 65 — on suspicion of drug trafficking and belonging to
a criminal group. [@1] The arrests came in three court-authorised searches of
homes in the town. [@1] The operation shut three drug sales points in flats near
the zona inglesa, the town's nightlife area. [@1] Three of the 11 were brought
before Benidorm's on-duty investigating courts, and the court ordered the main
suspect remanded in custody. [@1]

The searches yielded more than 550 grams of cocaine, 34 grams of marijuana, 43
grams of hashish and four grams of pink cocaine, known as tusi. [@1] Officers
also seized more than 4,000 euros in cash, 18 high-end mobile phones and
equipment allegedly used to prepare and distribute the drugs. [@1]

The investigation began after police received information about a family clan
said to be selling cocaine, hashish and marijuana from properties near the zona
inglesa. [@1] Investigators identified two men as the main organisers and found
that three homes were used for small exchanges of drugs for money inside the
flats and in the street outside. [@1] The group changed its schedules, routes
and routines to make police surveillance harder. [@1] Police described the
network as organised, with specialised roles among its members and a common way
of operating for profit. [@2]
