Immigration police raided a three-storey detached house in Soi Khu Bon 27, Soi 14, in Tha Raeng,
Bang Khen district, at 1pm on 19 October 2026, after residents complained about a strong cannabis
smell coming from the property. [@1] Officers of the Department of Thai Traditional and Alternative
Medicine joined the operation, which followed about two months of evidence gathering. [@1] Inside,
they found more than five tonnes of cannabis flowers packed in plastic and black bags across all
three floors, along with a ventilation system installed to carry the smell outside. [@1,2]

Five foreign nationals were found sorting the flowers for packing and delivery: one Vietnamese
national and four Myanmar nationals. [@1,2] The Myanmar workers said they were paid 200 to 300 baht
a day, while investigators believe the Vietnamese detainee supervised the operation, which they said
ran in a company-style system. [@1,2] A Thai national had signed the rental agreement before the
foreigners moved in, and police said the house, covering about 100 square wah, had been used as a
sorting and packing point for more than a year. [@1]

The operators held a cannabis licence issued in Nong Bua Lamphu province, which did not cover
activities in Bangkok; selling or processing cannabis flowers needs a permit for the operating
location. [@1,2] Breaking the rule carries up to one year in prison, a fine of up to 20,000 baht, or
both. [@1,2] The cannabis will be laboratory-tested to establish whether it was grown in Thailand or
imported, while police investigate the source, the intended customers and everyone connected with the
operation. [@1,2]
