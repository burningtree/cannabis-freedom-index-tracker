On 29 October 2026 the Tainan District Court sentenced a woman surnamed Tang to
10 years and six months in prison for jointly transporting cannabis, a
second-level narcotic in Taiwan, and the sentence can still be appealed. [@1]
Judges convicted her of transporting a second-level narcotic and of importing
controlled goods, and imposed the sentence on the more serious transport charge.
[@1] The court ordered the 72 packets of cannabis confiscated and destroyed.
[@2,4]

Tang had agreed to receive an international parcel and handle its paperwork for
a man she knew through a messaging app as Shih Chen-wei, hoping the arrangement
would help her obtain a NT$500,000 loan. [@3,4] Following his instructions, she
downloaded the EZ WAY customs app and used her younger brother's name and phone
number to clear the parcel. [@1] The box of biscuits and sundries flew from
Bangkok and reached Taipei Customs on 5 May 2026, where officers opened it and
found 72 packets of cannabis with a net weight of about 2,030 grams. [@1,4] None
of it reached the market. [@3,4]

Investigators let the courier deliver the parcel to her address as planned, and
Tang was arrested when she came out to collect it. [@1] She denied the charge,
saying Shih had told her that a side business in online selling would show she
could repay the loan, and that she did not know the parcel contained cannabis.
[@1,4] The court did not accept her defence, finding that a stranger offering a
loan in exchange for receiving parcels was highly suspicious and that she had
foreseen the parcel might contain drugs but let it happen. [@3,4] It said the
2,030 grams were not a small quantity and, had they reached the market, would
have accelerated the spread of drugs. [@1]

The judges said they also weighed Tang's attitude after the offence and her living and financial circumstances when setting the sentence. [@4]
