On 19 October 2026 the Lviv regional prosecutor's office and Lviv region police said they had stopped a group of seven people who grew cannabis and kept it for sale. [@1,2]

From May to October 2026, four men aged 53, 56, 59 and 63 set up a plantation of almost 1.8 hectares in the Trostianets community of Stryi district, which Pika places in the village of Lub'yana. [@1,2] They cleared the ground, put in irrigation and used fertiliser and growth stimulants. [@1] A second plot, of 0.038 hectares, was in a gardening association near Lviv, which Pika names as Solonka, where three of the suspects lived. [@1,2]

The other three were a family: a 46-year-old man, his 44-year-old wife and their 19-year-old son, a student at a Lviv college. [@1,2] Investigators say they handled wholesale and retail sales, including shipments to other regions of Ukraine. [@1]

Officers searched 14 places, the plots and the suspects' homes. [@1,2] On the plantation they found 30 cannabis plants, and in all they seized more than 30 kg of cannabis packed in bags, boxes, wraps, plastic containers and jars. [@1] Pika described the plant seizure as dozens of plants. [@2] They also took five vehicles, a Mercedes-Benz, a Mitsubishi Lancer Evo X, a Skoda Yeti, a Renault Kangoo and an Opel, plus scales, vacuum sealers, phones and notes. [@1,2] The cash was almost 760,000 hryvnias, more than 5,800 US dollars, 920 euros, 1,350 zlotys and 600 pounds. [@1,2] The prosecutor's office put the value of the plant material at up to 15 million hryvnias. [@1,2] 032.ua said the plantation was destroyed. [@1]

All seven were detained and notified of suspicion of acquiring and storing narcotics for sale in an especially large amount, acting together, and of sowing and growing cannabis. [@1,2] 032.ua said they face up to 12 years in prison with confiscation of property. [@1] The pre-trial investigation was continuing. [@1,2]
