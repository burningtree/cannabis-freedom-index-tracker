A Bahrain appeal court reduced a man’s sentence from fifteen years to ten in a case involving cannabis seeds and several drugs, The Daily Tribune reported on 24 January 2026. It retained a 5,000-dinar fine and confiscation of the seized items.[@1]

## The case involved seeds and a home search

Prosecutors alleged possession of cannabis seeds for trafficking and hashish and pregabalin for personal use. Investigators described a home-growing operation. Laboratory examinations identified viable cannabis seeds and several drugs among the seized material.[@1]

The newspaper said the searches uncovered cultivation equipment and that a phone examination found seed-ordering and growing material. The appeal reduced the prison term while leaving the fine and confiscation orders in place.[@1]

## Viable seeds were covered by the law

Bahrain’s [2007 drug law](https://cannabisfreedom.fyi/history/bahrain-narcotics-law-2007/) covers listed plants, their parts and their seeds, and defines cultivation to include handling seeds. It places personal-use acquisition and growing within the personal-use offence.[@2]

The original plant schedule expressly names cannabis. Its exemption covers fibre and seeds treated so they cannot germinate. Viable seeds remain within the law’s plant controls.[@3]

That distinction connects the laboratory finding to the legislation: whether a seed can grow matters to the exemption, while the alleged purpose of holding it matters to the offence.[@2,3]

## Trafficking and personal use had separate penalties

The trafficking offence carries death or life imprisonment and a fine of 5,000–50,000 dinars, with death required in specified aggravated cases. Personal possession or growing carries imprisonment and a fine up to 3,000 dinars.[@2]

Providing drugs for use without payment also has a separate offence carrying up to seven years and a fine. The law therefore distinguishes several purposes and activities within its drug offences.[@2]

Courts must confiscate seized drugs and equipment used for offences, subject to the protection of innocent third parties’ rights. Those confiscation rules operate alongside the prison and fine provisions.[@2]

## Synthetic substances also came under control

Bahrain [added synthetic cannabinoids to its narcotics list in 2016](https://cannabisfreedom.fyi/history/bahrain-synthetic-cannabinoids-2016/), through a decision that also added other drugs and a precursor to the schedules.[@4]

The appeal followed a [2019 home-growing case](/report/bahrain-home-growing-sentence-2019/) under the same principal law.[@5] The [Bahrain profile](https://cannabisfreedom.fyi/country/bh/) explains the penalties and the distinct rules for plants, viable seeds and treatment.
