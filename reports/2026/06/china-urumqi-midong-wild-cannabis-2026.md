Officers from the Midong District public security sub-bureau's narcotics brigade
and the Baiyanghe Kazakh Ethnic Township police station found a large stand of
wild cannabis on hillsides around Baiyanghe in Urumqi, Xinjiang, during a
routine foot patrol of hills, scrub and slopes. [@2] Officers counted more than
200,000 plants growing in one continuous patch. [@2] The brigade called in the
township government and the Midong District People's Procuratorate for a joint
clearance. [@2] The patrol that found the stand took place on 24 June 2026. [@3]

Officers pulled the plants up by the root, counted them one by one and sealed
them, photographing and filming the work as evidence. [@1] Excavators dug out
the root systems of the continuous stands, while officers pulled by hand the
plants on slopes and in rock crevices that machines could not reach. [@3] The
work was held to the standard of “not one plant, not one root, not one hidden
danger”. [@2]

Hemp has been grown in Xinjiang for more than 2,500 years. [@1] Seed scattered
over that time has reproduced in the wild, forming stable stands. [@1] Similar
clearances have become routine across the region. [@1] At Baiyanghe, officers
used the uprooted plants to show herders how to recognise cannabis and to
explain the law. [@3]
