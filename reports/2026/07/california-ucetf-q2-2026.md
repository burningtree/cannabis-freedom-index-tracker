The state says its task force goes after unlicensed cannabis operations tied to organised crime,
environmental harm and threats to public safety. [@2] From April to June 2026, the state's Unified Cannabis Enforcement Task Force and local
partners seized or destroyed 63,204 pounds (about 28.7 tonnes) of cannabis, valued at more than USD
104 million, and eradicated 89,257 plants. [@1] They arrested 24 people, confiscated 17 firearms and
took USD 220,923 in cash, in ten counties. [@1]

## Where

Kern County saw the most: 25,122 pounds destroyed and 26,442 plants eradicated, followed by Alameda,
Butte, Los Angeles and Stanislaus. [@1] The largest single operation, led by the Department of Fish
and Wildlife from 14 May to 3 June, served 26 search warrants in Tulare, Kern and Los Angeles counties
and eradicated nearly 24,000 plants. [@1] At 13 of those sites, officers found or suspected banned or
unregistered pesticides. [@1] In Ventura County, a months-long investigation ended in searches at nine
places and 14 arrests. [@1]

## Since 2022

Governor Gavin Newsom set up the task force in 2022; it is co-chaired by the Department of Cannabis
Control and the Department of Fish and Wildlife. [@1,2] By its own count it has since seized and
destroyed more than 841,000 pounds of cannabis, including 1.3 million plants, executed more than 750
search warrants in 29 counties, and made 100 arrests. [@1]
