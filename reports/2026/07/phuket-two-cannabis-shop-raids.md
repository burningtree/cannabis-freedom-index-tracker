Officials of Thailand's Department of Provincial Administration, with Phuket provincial and public
health officers, raided two cannabis shops on 11 July 2026 that were alleged to have kept selling
while their licences were suspended, arresting their operators and seizing goods officials valued
at more than 500,000 baht. [@1,2]

The Deputy Interior Minister, Polapee Suwannachawi, and the deputy director-general of the Department
of Provincial Administration led the department's criminal-case unit in a joint operation with the
Phuket provincial administration office, the provincial public health office and the Thalang district
offices, after reports that shops were selling during their licence suspension. [@1] Teams
entered the two targets at the same time: the shop in Thalang was found open
and still selling cannabis, and the shop in Mueang Phuket district showed the same alleged offence.
[@1,2] The suspects and the seized goods were handed to local investigators for prosecution. [@1]

The suspects face charges of selling a controlled herb, cannabis, for commerce without a licence
under the Thai Traditional Medicine Protection and Promotion Act B.E. 2542, which carries up to one
year in prison or a fine of up to 20,000 baht, and food-labelling offences under the Food Act
B.E. 2522 with fines up to 30,000 baht. [@1,2] The deputy minister said the state would not let
tourist areas become bases for illegal activity, and the department pledged nationwide inspections
of cannabis shops. [@1,2] The ministry of public health had separately introduced tougher penalties
restricting cannabis to medical use. [@2]
