On 20 August 2026 the 17th criminal chamber of the Berlin Regional Court
sentenced ten men, aged 25 to 51, for smuggling cannabis. [@1] The three main
defendants were convicted of gang dealing in cannabis in seven cases, and the
court treated seven of the ten as members of the group. [@1]

Mohamad G., 48, whom the court called a leading figure, received two aggregate sentences totalling 15 years and six
months, including an older judgment. [@1] Seven years and six months of that
covered the first two acts, and eight years the later five. [@2] Andrej F., 45, a sauna trader from Werneuchen, received
eight years and nine months. [@1,2] Dmitrij P., 41, the only one to confess
in full, received six years and six months. [@1] The other seven received
between one year and six months and five years, for aiding or for dealing,
and two of those sentences were suspended. [@1] G. and F. must each pay 2.34
million euros in confiscated proceeds. [@1]

The chamber found that between August 2023 and November 2024 the defendants
brought 7.7 tonnes of marijuana into Germany in seven shipments, to sell in
Berlin. [@1] Six shipments of 1.1 tonnes each came by sea from Canada, hidden
in cavities in wood loads and moved through front companies. [@1] The origin
of the first shipment could not be established, and investigators seized the
last. [@1] Märkische Oderzeitung, from courtroom 700 at the Moabit criminal
court, says each load was 1.1 tonnes and arrived at F.'s properties in
Werneuchen and Leuenberg. [@2] The last six came through Hamburg and the
Dreilinden customs office, and police stopped the unloading at Leuenberg on
27 November 2024. [@2]

Presiding judge Nina Sommer said F. was paid at least 390,000 euros a delivery
and rejected his claim that the wood was for sauna equipment. [@2] A
28-year-old Polish defendant, who had meant to take about a third of a
shipment at Leuenberg, got five years for dealing in six cases and stayed in
custody, while P. was released from remand on strict reporting conditions.
[@2] Sommer called the case international organised crime, not a minor matter
under the rules that allow some cannabis use. [@1] The judgment, case 517 KLs
22/25, is not final and can be appealed to the Federal Court of Justice. [@1]
