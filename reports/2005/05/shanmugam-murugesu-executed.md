Shanmugam s/o Murugesu, aged 38, was executed at 6 a.m. on 13 May 2005 at Changi Prison. [@1] He
had been sentenced to death after importing 1,029.8 grams of cannabis at the Tuas Checkpoint on 29
August 2003. [@3] Amnesty International said he was hanged despite a sustained public campaign and
that authorities rejected his lawyer's application on 11 May to stay the execution and convene a
Constitutional Court review. [@1]

## Mandatory sentence

Under Singapore's Misuse of Drugs Act, importing more than 500 grams of cannabis carries a mandatory
death penalty. [@2] Immigration officers found six packets of cannabis in his motorcycle when he
entered from Johor; the Court of Appeal upheld his conviction in January 2005 after he admitted in
recorded statements that he knew he was carrying the drugs for payment. [@3] President S.R. Nathan
rejected a clemency petition before the hanging. [@2]

## Family and protest

Murugesu's 14-year-old twin sons, Gopalan and Krishnan, appealed publicly for his life, and civic
groups held vigils and an online petition. [@2] Lawyer M. Ravi called the execution a "cold blooded
murder" but said the campaign had raised awareness of capital punishment. [@2] Amnesty International
described the case as rare public dissent against government policy in Singapore. [@1] The Home
Affairs Ministry defended the death penalty as part of a system that kept drug abuse rates low. [@2]
