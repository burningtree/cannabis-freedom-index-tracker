The Drug Enforcement Administration said federal, state and local authorities were eradicating more
than 20,000 marijuana plants in the Coconino National Forest in mid-August 2005, in the latest of
three separate growing operations found in the same area since 24 July. [@1] The agency valued that
cultivation at over $30 million and called it one of the largest of its kind found in Arizona. [@1]

## Takedown north of Strawberry

Associated Press reporting on 16 August described a mile-long farm in a remote ravine on the
southern part of the forest north of Strawberry, after a Gila County narcotics task force had
watched the site since July. [@2] Officers confronted heavily armed men in the garden on Monday
15 August and arrested four people, according to court paperwork summarised by the wire service. [@2]
The DEA named them as Jesus Castillo-Malendrez, Gerardo Manzo-Pulido, Oscar Nunez-Medina and David
Valencia-Gonzalez, all from Mexico, and said federal conspiracy charges were filed on 16 August. [@1]

Special Agent in Charge Timothy J. Landrum said international traffickers had exploited public land
to cultivate thousands of plants. [@1] The Forest Service's Robin Poague warned that large-scale
cultivation on national forests posed hazards to visitors and environmental damage. [@1]
