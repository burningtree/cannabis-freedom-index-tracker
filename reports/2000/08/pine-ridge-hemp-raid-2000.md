Alex White Plume was awakened about 6 a.m. on 24 August 2000 by a helicopter over his home near
Manderson on the Pine Ridge Indian Reservation. [@1] Outside, roughly 25 armed agents from the Drug
Enforcement Administration, the Federal Bureau of Investigation, the U.S. Marshals Service and the
Northern Plains Safe Trails Drug Task Force prepared to cut the industrial-hemp crop he had tended
since spring for a Slim Buttes Land-Use Association housing project. [@1,2]

## Plants removed, no arrests

Agents cut and trucked away 3,782 plants from White Plume’s 1.5-acre plot, according to Rapid City
Journal reporting republished by CannabisNews. [@1] They also raided a second 50-foot-square plot
near Slim Buttes marked as an Oglala Sioux Tribe Department of Agriculture experimental site. [@1]
Indianz.com said almost 4,000 plants were taken across the two farms and that no one was arrested,
although federal law allows ten years to life for cultivating 1,000 or more plants. [@2]

The Slim Buttes group had publicised the planting since April and invited tribal police to view the
fields in July; a Bureau of Indian Affairs investigator had sent a sample to the University of
Mississippi after a June overflight. [@1] Tribal leaders passed an industrial-hemp ordinance in
1998, but U.S. law does not distinguish hemp from marijuana without a federal permit. [@2] White
Plume said the hemp was destined for building materials on the reservation; officials declined to
comment on the case in the opened reports. [@1,2]
