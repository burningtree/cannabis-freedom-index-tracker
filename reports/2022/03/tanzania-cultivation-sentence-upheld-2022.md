Tanzania's Court of Appeal upheld Hamis Muhibu Abdallah's thirty-year sentence for cultivating cannabis in March 2022.[@1] Although it quashed a separate possession conviction, the cultivation sentence remained because the court found other evidence supported his retracted confession.[@1]

## Police found a field of 535 plants

Police acting on information went to a farm in Nanyumbu District in May 2017.[@1] Officers and an independent witness found 535 cannabis plants, which were uprooted and taken as exhibits.[@1]

Hamis and his younger brother were charged, but the brother was acquitted at trial.[@1] Hamis received concurrent thirty-year sentences for cultivation and possession, and his earlier appeal was unsuccessful.[@1]

The [2015 drug law](https://cannabisfreedom.fyi/history/tanzania-drug-control-act-2015/) provided at least thirty years for unlawful cultivation of prohibited plants.[@2] The appeal therefore concerned evidence carrying a sentence measured in decades.[@1,2]

## The confession was disputed but supported by other evidence

Hamis challenged the evidence linking him to the farm and withdrew the admission attributed to him.[@1] The appeal court considered whether that admission had independent support.[@1]

It relied on evidence that he had led police to the field and on testimony from an independent witness who knew the land and had seen him developing it into a farm.[@1] Witness evidence also placed him at the scene growing cannabis.[@1]

The court found that those details supported the confession and left no reasonable doubt about the cultivation offence.[@1] It rejected his defence that the farm was not his.[@1]

## The same plants did not prove a second offence

The judges distinguished evidence of cultivation from evidence of the separate possession count.[@1] Plants uprooted from the farm proved the growing allegation, but did not establish the additional possession charge as presented.[@1]

They therefore quashed the possession conviction and its corresponding thirty-year sentence.[@1] The ruling kept the two offences separate instead of allowing the same allegation about the growing plants to establish both.[@1]

## One surviving count still meant thirty years

The two sentences had run concurrently, so removing the possession sentence left the cultivation term in place.[@1] The appeal was otherwise dismissed, and Hamis remained subject to thirty years in prison.[@1]

For growers in [Tanzania](https://cannabisfreedom.fyi/country/tz/), the case showed the practical force of the cultivation minimum.[@1,2] Correcting one charge changed the convictions on the record, while the proven growing offence continued to determine the time in custody.[@1]
