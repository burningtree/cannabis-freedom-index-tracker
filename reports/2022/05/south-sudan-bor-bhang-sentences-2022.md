A Bor cannabis case ended with prison terms for five young men and fines for five others in May 2022. The police commissioner told Eye Radio that the ten, aged eighteen to twenty-two, had been found smoking bhang during a night patrol. Their reported outcomes differed within the same case.[@1]

## The report describes both custody and fines

One man received a year in prison, four received six months, and five were fined 50,000 South Sudanese pounds each. Police also arrested a supplier. The report did not assign a separate cannabis quantity to each young man.[@1]

Those outcomes fit the range of options in the [2008 Penal Code](https://cannabisfreedom.fyi/history/south-sudan-penal-code-2008/). Personal possession and consuming a dangerous drug can bring up to five years in prison, a fine, or both. The statute therefore allows a court to choose a fine while retaining the power to impose custody.[@2]

The legal distinction matters when reading an arrest headline. The personal-use offence covers acquiring or possessing drugs, smoking or otherwise consuming them, and growing or producing them for oneself. It places those activities within the same sentencing provision.[@2]

## A supplier faces a different offence

Supplying, selling, delivering or transporting a dangerous drug falls within the separate dealing offence. So does growing or possessing it for dealing. The ordinary maximum is fourteen years, with a fine also available.[@2]

An earlier [Torit police report in 2019](/report/south-sudan-torit-bangi-arrests-2019/) described a different kind of allegation: a man and woman allegedly transported four sacks of bangi from Ketire into Torit. Police reported each sack as 100 kg and said the suspects would be sent to court after investigation.[@3]

That account recorded an investigation. The Bor report, by contrast, gave sentencing outcomes after a court appearance. Keeping those stages separate lets the history show both what police alleged and what punishment was later reported.[@1,3]

## Treatment is another power in the code

The personal-possession and use provision also lets the court require treatment where it establishes drug abuse or addiction. Treatment can accompany or replace the ordinary sentence. The power depends on the court’s findings in the person’s case.[@2]

For the [South Sudan profile](https://cannabisfreedom.fyi/country/ss/), the Bor case supplies a concrete example of the fine and prison alternatives operating together. The law’s maximum sentence describes the court’s power; the reported six-month, one-year and fine outcomes show the different punishments imposed in this case.[@1,2]
