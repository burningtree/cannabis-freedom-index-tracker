On 30 October 2006 Attorney General Bill Lockyer said California’s Campaign Against Marijuana
Planting (CAMP) had eradicated 1,675,681 illegally cultivated cannabis plants during the 2006
season, a record that exceeded the previous year by 540,989 plants. [@1,2] CAMP, led by the
Department of Justice’s Bureau of Narcotic Enforcement with federal and local partners, valued the
removed crop at more than $6.7 billion. [@1,2]

## Season totals

Lockyer said teams conducted 477 raids in 34 counties during the traditional outdoor season from
late July through early October. [@1,2] Officers made 27 arrests and seized 29 weapons. [@1,2] About
80 percent of the plants were on public land such as state and national parks and forests; the rest
were on private property including ranches and vineyards. [@1,2] The attorney general’s office said
five regional teams worked the season and reported no officer-involved shootings or fatalities.
