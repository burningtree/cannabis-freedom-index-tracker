Officers from the National Drug Centre (NPC), Czechia's anti-narcotics unit,
charged six people with trafficking drugs between Slovakia and Czechia. [@1]
Four of the accused are in pre-trial custody and face 10 to 18 years in prison
if convicted. [@1] A fifth is being investigated at liberty and faces two to 10
years. [@1] Police said the group's head was a foreigner who brought drugs from
Slovakia repeatedly, up to twice a month and in kilogram quantities. [@2] One
Czech accused ordered the shipments and decided where they went, and another
worked as a courier, investigators said. [@2]

At the home of one of the accused, officers found an indoor cannabis grow with
modern equipment and seized 100 plants; police said the crop was not finished
when they intervened. [@2] Detectives were checking whether the same man took
part in the rest of the methamphetamine and precursor trade. [@2] In Slovakia,
police seized 25 kg of hashish, a cannabis resin. [@1] The operation also took
100 kg of methamphetamine, 5 kg of cocaine and 32 kg of pseudoephedrine-based
medicine. [@2] Police estimated the methamphetamine would have sold for 100
million koruna at 1,000 koruna a gram. [@2]

The case, code-named Vivaldi, was described by the NPC's director, Jakub
Frydrych, and Milan Jonis, head of the Slovak anti-drug unit, as a record
seizure of drugs of that size in Czechia and Slovakia. [@1] Czech detectives
worked on the case for nearly a year and Slovak officers for about six months.
