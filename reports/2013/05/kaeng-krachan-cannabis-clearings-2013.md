Under the park's Operation Tanawsri, Kaeng Krachan National Park chief Chaiwat
Limlikhitaksorn sent almost 100 forest rangers, soldiers, border police and
narcotics officers to Jai Paen Din on the Thai-Myanmar border, using two
helicopters. [@1] They were to verify and search 16 target areas where people
had felled trees, burned forest, hunted and cleared land for field crops mixed
with cannabis. [@1] Governor Monthien Thongnit, senator Sumol Sutawiriyawat and
Chaiwat made the trip to the summit by helicopter from the Ministry of Natural
Resources and Environment. [@2] The inspection took place on 1 May 2013. [@2]

They found forest burned black from the foot of the mountain to the summit, with
many large trees felled, some several arm-spans around. [@2] Upland rice (khao
rai) had been planted on part of the cleared ground. [@2] Cannabis had been sown
in the rice holes and was found in almost every encroached area. [@2] The size
of the cleared area could not yet be measured because the mountain is steep.
[@2] From the air each patch looked small, but on the ground it was more than 10
to 20 rai (1.6 to 3.2 hectares). [@2] The cleared forest is watershed for the
Kaeng Krachan dam and the Phetchaburi River. [@1]

The officers found no offenders. [@2] The intruders stole two small chainsaw
engines that rangers had left near the helicopter landing site. [@2] Chaiwat
said the intruders were hill people who knew the forest better than his officers
and had probably been watching them. [@2] The governor said the offenders were
probably ethnic minorities living along the Myanmar border, about 5 km from Jai
Paen Din. [@2] He said Thai citizens and ethnic people living in Thailand or
coming from neighbouring countries would be prosecuted strictly under the law.
[@1] The survey found that less forest had been encroached than in every
previous year. [@2]
