On 7 October 2003 agents from the DEA’s Long Island district office and Nassau County police
detectives executed 18 search warrants in Nassau and Suffolk counties. [@1] They arrested nine people
and seized about 175 pounds of marijuana, roughly $700,000 in proceeds or substituted proceeds, five
motor vehicles and 17 handguns, including a pen gun, a shotgun and a rifle. [@1]

## The Oyster Bay ring

The investigation targeted a network that imported and sold large quantities of marijuana. [@1] Task
force officers learned that Lester Huffer, a former Oyster Bay Chamber of Commerce president who sold
signs from Pine Hollow Road and played Santa at malls, was supplying the trade from his home and
store. [@1,2] Low-grade Mexican marijuana was nicknamed “Bob”; higher-grade hydroponic cannabis sold
wholesale for about $3,200 to $3,500 a pound under the name “Phil”. [@1,2]

Retired pharmacist John J. Donovan, arrested with almost 100 pounds of marijuana and more than
$350,000 in cash, had driven loads coast to coast in a minivan for about two years, the DEA said.
[@1] Six defendants faced Nassau County marijuana sale or possession charges punishable by up to 15
years; three faced conspiracy counts. [@1] Nassau District Attorney Denis Dillon said the operation
also kept prior felony offenders and a large cache of weapons off the streets. [@1,2]
