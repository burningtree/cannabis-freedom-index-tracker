On Wednesday 17 September 2003, Drug Enforcement Administration agents, Illinois State Police,
Cook County sheriff's officers and the Illinois National Guard seized about 6,000 marijuana plants
from six concealed plots in the Cap Sauer's Holdings section of the Cook County forest preserve near
Palos Park. [@1] The plants stood 5 to 12 feet tall in unincorporated Palos Township at McCarthy Road
and 104th Avenue. [@1]

## Burn on site

Officers found the farms about a mile north of 123rd Street between 104th Avenue and Wolf Road after
airborne surveillance. [@1] They cut the plants down and burned them where they grew. [@1] DEA Chicago
special agent in charge Richard Sanders said growers had damaged trees and left garbage in otherwise
pristine woodland. [@1] Cook County Sheriff Michael Sheahan said hikers could encounter armed
cultivators in such sites. [@1] DEA spokesman Gary Boertleen said no arrests had been made by
Wednesday but investigators were following several leads. [@1] Sanders said the raid was part of the
agency's Domestic Cannabis Eradication/Suppression Program and that more than 20,000 plants had
already been seized in northeastern Illinois that year. [@1]

## Aftermath

Palos Park Police Chief Joe Miller told the Tribune that publicity about the burn drew people hoping
to salvage usable cannabis from the area, creating parking problems and alarming nearby residents.
[@2] Miller said the seekers visited daily even though authorities said everything found had been
destroyed. [@2]
