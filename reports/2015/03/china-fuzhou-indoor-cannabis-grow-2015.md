Fuzhou customs officers broke up a smuggling ring that brought cannabis into
China by international mail, seizing 4.5128 kg and destroying 332 plants at
three indoor grow sites. [@1] All four suspects were arrested. [@1] They came
from Changle, Ningde and Fuqing. [@2] On 30 March 2015 the customs
anti-smuggling bureau transferred the case to prosecutors for review and
prosecution. [@3] The plants were grown in three rented flats in the Fenghu
Xincheng and Xiaoliu Xincun housing estates in Fuzhou. [@2] The flats were
fitted with ventilation ducts, ultraviolet lamps, air conditioning and
fluorescent lights. [@1] Some plants had grown over 1.7 metres and flowered.
[@1] The windows were boarded up to hide the operation. [@1] Customs officers
said the plants were a new American strain with THC content up to 20 per cent,
against 5 per cent in traditional varieties. [@1]

On 18 and 19 November 2014, officers at the customs post office X-rayed
international mail and found five parcels with abnormal images. [@2] Each parcel
held four packages disguised as food; the parcels weighed 4.937 kg gross and
4.5128 kg net, and tested positive for THC, cannabidiol and cannabinol. [@2] A
man from Changle surnamed Jiang had agreed with a contact in the United States
known as "Brother Long" to send five parcels from California in two batches to
four false recipients. [@2] Jiang asked his sister and two friends, surnamed
Huang and Tian, to collect them while he went to Hong Kong. [@2]

Officers watched a housing estate near the delivery address for a day and a
night and arrested Tian when he kept circling the mail room without collecting
the parcels. [@2] A bunch of keys on him led them to the Fenghu Xincheng grow
site. [@2] It was the first indoor cannabis cultivation case Fuzhou customs had
handled. [@1]
