The Drug Enforcement Administration said a seven-day eradication sweep on St. Croix removed almost
10,000 marijuana plants from 80 fields, at a pace of more than 1,400 plants a day, in what acting
special agent in charge Vito Salvatore Guarino called a record for the island. [@1] Gene Hawk, the
resident agent in charge in St. Croix, said plants ranged from small plots to twelve-foot-tall
stalks. [@1]

## Island-wide operation

Operation No Plant Left Behind covered St. Croix from Estate Cotton Valley in the east to Estate
White Lady in the west, north to Rust Op Twist and along the south-shore beaches. [@1] The DEA led
federal and local partners including the Virgin Islands Police Department, the Virgin Islands
National Guard, and the Puerto Rico National Guard. [@1] Hawk warned that marijuana trafficking
could bring long federal prison terms and noted that several people arrested after prior eradication
work on the island had been sentenced on 17 June 2008 to five years in the U.S. District Court for
the Virgin Islands. [@1]
