On 26 September 2025 Paraguay's anti-drug secretariat SENAD said Operation Nueva Alianza 52 had
ended after ten days of air and ground raids with Brazil's Federal Police in Amambay. [@1]

Teams working around Cerro 21, Cerro Kuatiá, Itapopó and Ombú cleared 285 hectares of cannabis and
burned 13,130 kilogrammes of marijuana ready for distribution. [@1,2] Adding estimated harvest from
the cleared fields, SENAD put 868 tonnes out of circulation, mainly bound for Brazil, and valued the
loss to organised crime at USD 130 million. [@1,2]

The operation dismantled 83 trafficking camps; SENAD said the plantations had required deforestation
on a scale it compared to 285 football pitches. [@1,2] Anti-drug prosecutor Rosana Coronel directed
the work with SENAD, the Federal Police, the Public Ministry and Paraguayan air force helicopters.
[@1,2] The accounts opened here name no arrests. [@1,2]
