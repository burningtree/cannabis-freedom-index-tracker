Paraguay's National Anti-Drug Secretariat (Senad) and Brazil's Federal Police closed the 53rd phase
of Operation Nueva Alianza on 31 October 2025, after ten days of raids by air and on foot in the
forests of Canindeyú, Caaguazú and Alto Paraná. [@1,3] The teams destroyed 309 hectares of cannabis
and dismantled 96 camps used as bases for producing and storing the drug. [@1] Inside the camps they
burned more than 39 tonnes of chopped and pressed marijuana. [@1,3] The agency's statement names no
arrests. [@1]

The work concentrated on 7 Montes, Pindó and the Morombí nature reserve, in Canindeyú. [@1] Paraguay's
air force flew the teams in and the public prosecutor's office took part. [@1] On the last day, Senad
minister Jalil Rachid and Brazil's ambassador, José Antonio Marcondes, followed the raids. [@1]

## The first count

On 24 October, with the operation still under way, Senad reported 98 hectares eradicated, a potential
294,000 kilograms of cannabis, 3,550 kilograms of chopped marijuana and 1,232 pressed, 12 camps and 11
rustic presses. [@2] In the Asentamiento Primero de Marzo, in Alto Paraná, agents found what the agency
called a laboratory-type industrial set-up for high-quality cannabis. [@2] At that stage it put the
loss to the organisations at more than USD 44 million; the final account says the phase kept at least
966,000 kilograms of cannabis from reaching Brazil, where technical estimates put the earnings it
would have generated at more than USD 144 million. [@1,2]

Rachid has warned that illegal cannabis production has stripped more than 5,000 hectares of forest in
Paraguay in two years. [@2] Neither statement says whether the cleared plots will be replanted. [@1,2]
