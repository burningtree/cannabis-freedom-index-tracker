Police in Yutian County in China's Xinjiang region have uprooted wild cannabis
and corn poppies, a Papaver-genus species the force classes as a drug-source
plant, the county public security bureau said. [@1] The bureau's anti-drug
department carried out the operation with the Tuogeri Gaizi police station. [@1]
The force called the work 禁种铲毒 (jinzhong chandu), or “ban cultivation, eradicate
drugs”. [@1] Officers said the removals eliminated the harm that drug-source
plants pose to society. [@1]

Officers searched farmland, disused courtyards and other places where drug
plants are likely to grow. [@1] They worked to a standard of leaving “not one
place unchecked, not one plant standing and no corner untouched”. [@1] The force
described the inspection as all-round and carpet-like. [@1]

During the sweep, officers explained to residents how to recognise poppies and
cannabis and described their harms. [@1] They publicised anti-drug laws and the
rules on banning cultivation and eradicating drug crops. [@1] They encouraged
residents to report illegal planting. [@1] Many residents said they would answer
the call and refuse to grow drug-source plants. [@1] The force said the
operation had strengthened residents' awareness of drugs and of the rules
against planting them. [@1]
