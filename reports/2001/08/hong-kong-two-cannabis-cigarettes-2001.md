Selling two cannabis cigarettes containing 0.24 g brought Chor Lui two months in prison. On 3 August 2001, Hong Kong’s High Court rejected his appeal: the amount was small, but the judge considered the commercial sale in a discotheque and his previous drug convictions serious.[@1]

## An undercover customer paid HK$30

Chor was 23 when sentenced. According to the admitted facts, another man approached him inside a New Territories discotheque with a customer, who was an undercover police officer. She asked the price, and Chor sold her two hand-rolled cigarettes for HK$30, returning change from a marked HK$100 note.[@1]

His lawyer said he supported his parents and had taken decoration work after his arrest. In mitigation, the court was told the discotheque had offered him HK$400 a day to sell drugs and that he had begun that day.[@1]

## The court distinguished a sale from possession

The magistrate started at three months and reduced the sentence by one third for Chor’s guilty plea. He rejected a suspended sentence despite a favourable background report and family support. Chor had two previous drug-possession convictions and a conviction for wounding.[@1]

On appeal, the judge stressed that selling for profit at a venue frequented by young people differed from an unpaid exchange between friends. He also rejected comparison with a co-defendant who received community service for simple possession: that was a different offence.[@1]

## Herbal cannabis still had a lower benchmark

The court applied the approach developed in [the 1995 sentencing review](https://cannabisfreedom.fyi/history/hong-kong-cannabis-tariff-review-1995/), which allowed adjustments for the lower THC concentrations then found in herbal cannabis compared with resin.[@2]

The appeal judge reasoned that the resin guideline of up to sixteen months below 2 kg suggested a four-month benchmark for herbal cannabis at the lowest band. Even so, two months after a guilty plea remained within the magistrate’s discretion. The appeal was dismissed.[@1]

## Later cases changed the comparison

A year earlier, [Phung Hai had won a reduction](/report/hong-kong-personal-cannabis-sentence-2000/) from six months to two for possessing 1.18 g. That case involved possession and earlier convictions; Chor’s involved an admitted commercial sale.[@3]

In [January 2023](https://cannabisfreedom.fyi/history/hong-kong-cannabis-sentencing-guidelines-2023/), the Court of Appeal placed herbal cannabis and resin on the same trafficking scale after accepting evidence that their THC concentrations had converged. The [Hong Kong profile](https://cannabisfreedom.fyi/country/hk/) describes the later rules on possession, supply and growing.[@4]
