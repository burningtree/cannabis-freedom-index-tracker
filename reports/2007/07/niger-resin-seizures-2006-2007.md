In 2006–2007 Niger became the most visible interception point on a newly identified drug
highway. Five seizures in the country netted a total of 5.7 tons of cannabis resin moving
along the trans-Saharan route from Morocco through Mauritania and Mali, across Niger
toward Chad and on into Egypt or Sudan.[@1]

The route was new and the volumes were industrial: Moroccan resin crossing the Sahara by
land, with Niger — a country with almost no domestic resin market — as the corridor.[@1]
The seizures put the corridor on the map for enforcement across the region, linking
Niger's statistics to a supply chain that starts in the Rif and ends in Cairo or
Khartoum.[@1]

Two decades on, Niger still reports resin alongside herbal cannabis in its seizure
figures, a trace of the transit trade the 2006–2007 interceptions first exposed.
