On the morning of 25 August 2020 more than 70 law-enforcement officers from the DEA Grand Junction
office and twelve federal, state and local agencies raided five marijuana cultivation sites spread
over several square miles north of Rifle, Colorado. [@1,2] The U.S. Forest Service had opened the
investigation with support from the DEA and Bureau of Land Management. [@1]

## Plants and arrests

Agents removed 16,436 marijuana plants and arrested six suspects who were living at the illegal
camps on a mix of public and private land northwest of Rifle Gap Reservoir. [@1,2] One suspect was
hurt during a foot chase in rough terrain, airlifted to hospital with non-life-threatening injuries.
[@1] DEA resident agent in charge Steven Knight told the Post Independent the bust was among the
largest illicit outdoor grows in Colorado that year. [@2] The six detained men were described as
Hispanic males; reporters said they faced manufacturing-with-intent charges and that no guns were
found. [@2]

## Environmental damage

Local reporting said growers had diverted stream water to irrigate the sites and that fertilisers
and pesticides on illegal grows elsewhere in the West can poison wildlife; carbofuran was not found
here but had appeared at a similar Colorado operation in 2019. [@2] The DEA said the case was
referred to the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the District of Colorado. [@1]
