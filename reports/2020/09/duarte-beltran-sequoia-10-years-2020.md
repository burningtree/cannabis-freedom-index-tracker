Marco Lizandro Duarte-Beltran, a 34-year-old from Michoacán, Mexico, drew a 10-year federal prison
term on 21 September 2020 after U.S. District Judge Dale A. Drozd sentenced him for conspiring to
manufacture and distribute marijuana on public land. [@1] Court documents said he had cultivated
1,082 marijuana plants in the Millwood Creek area of Sequoia National Forest for about four months
before officers apprehended him at the site following a two-month investigation. [@1]

## Armed gardener and forest damage

The U.S. Attorney's Office said Duarte-Beltran wore camouflage and carried a loaded revolver when
arrested. [@1] The grow had cut native vegetation, terraced three campsite areas, diverted water from
a federal source, and scattered irrigation tubing, fertilizers, pesticides, and trash through the
site. [@1] Judge Drozd ordered $12,644 in restitution to clean up the damage. [@1] A guilty-plea
report published before sentencing recorded the same plant count and restitution figure and noted
that the mandatory minimum term was 10 years. [@2]
