Police arrested investigative reporter Ivan Golunov on Tsvetnoy Boulevard in central Moscow on 6 June 2019, while he was on his way to meet other journalists. [@3] Officers alleged that they found mephedrone in his backpack and charged him with intent to distribute drugs; Golunov said the drugs were not his. [@3]

Police published photographs suggesting that his flat contained a drug laboratory, then acknowledged that only one of the photographs had been taken there. [@3] Kommersant, Vedomosti and RBK published a joint front-page statement supporting him. [@1] The criminal case was closed on 11 June, five days after his arrest. [@4]

Five former officers were subsequently convicted of fabricating evidence and other offences in the case, receiving initial prison sentences of five to twelve years in May 2021. [@4] Their sentences were reduced on appeal; on 11 October 2022, the Supreme Court rejected further appeals by four of them. [@4]

The case prompted debate about Russia’s drug laws, but on 20 June 2019 President Vladimir Putin rejected softening Article 228, including for small quantities. [@2]
